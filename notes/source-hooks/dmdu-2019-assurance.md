**Why delegation-assurance cares.** DAP runs in two phases: a design phase where the plan, the monitoring programme, and the contingent actions are specified, and an implementation phase where monitoring runs and contingent actions fire. That is the developer/deployer division of evidential labour with different labels, worked out in a field that has had to make it survive contact with regulators and public budgets. DMDU also types its actions rather than leaving "respond appropriately" undefined: anticipatory actions are mitigating, hedging, seizing, exploiting, or shaping; contingent ones are defensive, corrective, capitalising, or reassessment. If DA wants a taxonomy of what a deployer owes on receiving a signal, this is a tested one to borrow or argue with.

**Why evidentiary-assurance cares.** Signposts specify what must be monitored and triggers specify the level at which action fires, both fixed *before* implementation rather than reconstructed afterwards. That is a record-adequacy regime, and it answers the Robodebt-shaped question of what a party with standing could have known and when. The lead-time rule matters here too: a trigger set at the tipping point leaves no time to act, so an assurance record that only shows the threshold being crossed is evidence of failure rather than of oversight.

**One structural caution.** DMDU assumes the parties want the plan to succeed and are arguing about how. Both assurance papers deal with settings where a party may benefit from the record being thin. Assumption-Based Planning's "load-bearing assumption" is an honest-actor construct: it asks which assumptions would sink the plan if false, not which ones a party has an interest in leaving untested. Do not import the framework without marking that gap; it is arguably where these papers add something DMDU does not have.

**House-style note.** `load-bearing assumption` is now permitted as the Assumption-Based Planning term of art (Dewar et al. 1993), capped at **two uses per paper** and enforced by `check-style.py`. Every other use of "load-bearing" is still banned. If a draft needs the idea more often, write "critical assumption" or name the assumption.
