**Why evidentiary-assurance cares.** EA's fourth verdict asks whether any named forum has the access, competence, independence, and remedial power to review a contested action. Ho et al. report a case, via the NYT, where a district attorney's office used AI to keep individuals in jail with filings allegedly full of hallucinations while the public defender's office had no comparable tools. That is a forum-capacity failure of a kind EA's `J_C(f)` is built to express: a forum exists and is formally competent, but one party cannot actually mount the review. Worth considering as a second real case alongside Robodebt, though it is thinner: Robodebt has a Royal Commission report behind it, this has a newspaper report behind an editors' introduction.

**Why delegation-assurance might care.** The feature contains Hadfield on legal infrastructure, including registration of frontier models and of AI agents, and regulatory markets. Agent registration is directly adjacent to DA's authorization layer, and DA's deferred institutional-mode worked case may want it. Laufer et al. on whether obligations should fall on developers or downstream deployers is relevant to DA's grant-relative non-amplification material. **Chase both as primary sources; don't cite the introduction for them.**

**Adversarial pragmatics:** framing only. The Guha paper in the same feature is the one AP wants. See `notes/source-hooks/guha-2026-benchmark-institutions.md`.
